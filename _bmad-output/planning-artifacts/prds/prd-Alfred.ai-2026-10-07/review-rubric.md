# PRD Quality Review — alfred.ai

*Reviewed 2026-10-08 against `prd-validation-checklist.md`. Stakes are calibrated to a solo hobby/portfolio project with a short PRD as the target.*

## Overall verdict
This is a strong PRD for its stakes. It has a clear thesis: a learning project where the user writes the agent loop himself, and where the personality and a code-enforced trust model are what make it distinctive. Decisions are stated plainly, scope is honest, and the counter-metrics show real thinking. The risk is in done-ness at the edges that matter most. The trust model's classifier has no rule for compound shell commands. The 60-second voice demo (SM-1) has no latency budget. And the canonical example dialogues that FR-1 makes acceptance references break FR-1's own 3-sentence bound. All three can be fixed in a paragraph each, and they should be fixed before story creation.

## Decision-readiness — strong
Decisions read as decisions, not considerations. Examples: "Yalnızca Claude, API anahtarıyla. Claude Pro aboneliği kullanılmaz" (§5), the rejected Claude Code subprocess path with its reasons (addendum, "Reddedilen yol"), fail-closed classification (FR-12), and push-to-talk over a wake word (§7.2). Trade-offs name what was given up. "Gizlilik, bilinçli risk … Yasak klasör ve maskeleme yoktur" (§5) is a good example of a cost that is owned rather than smoothed over. The Open Questions in §9 are mostly real and are tagged with the phase that owns them ("(mimari)").

### Findings
- **[medium]** An open question the PRD's author can answer today (§9 Q3) — "Emre'nin makinesinde GPU var mı?" is a fact, not an open question. Its answer decides the STT engine (addendum: "Etkileşimli bir hız için GPU gerekiyor") and therefore whether M3 and SM-1 are feasible as written. *Fix:* Answer it in the PRD. If there is no GPU, say whether the demo accepts slower STT or uses a CPU engine such as Vosk with an accuracy risk.
- **[low]** The M1 checkpoint does not say what gets cut (§7.1) — "M1 dört haftada bitmezse kapsam daraltılır" names the trigger but not the cut order. *Fix:* Add one line, for example: "önce YouTube (FR-11), sonra ses (M3); demo CLI ile yapılır."

## Substance over theater — strong
There is no persona theater: one user, named, with JTBD lines that each drive features (the "Güven" JTBD drives §4.5, and "Kurucu olarak" drives §5 "Kendi kodu"). The Vision could not be swapped into another PRD. The Batman's Alfred voice, the safety word, and the "learn by writing it" framing are specific. The Non-Goals in §6 block a real scope trap ("OpenClaw ya da Goose'a rakip bir ürün değildir"). There is no NFR boilerplate section at all. That is correct for these stakes, but it leaves the gap noted under Done-ness. The humor rule is bounded ("en fazla bir kısa cümledir") and has a counter-metric (SM-C3), which keeps it from being decoration.

## Strategic coherence — strong
The thesis is explicit: build the agent loop, tool layer and trust model yourself, with personality as the differentiator (§1, §5). Prioritization follows from it. M1 is the CLI decision loop, M2 is the trust model, and voice and YouTube come later (§7.1). That is thesis-first, not easy-first. The primary metrics validate the thesis (SM-1 is the demo, SM-2 is the DDD/agent write-up), and the counter-metrics SM-C1 and SM-C2 guard exactly the trade-offs a solo developer would be tempted to make. The MVP scope is clearly the experience/demo kind, and its scope logic matches.

### Findings
- **[medium]** The thing that "makes alfred alfred" has no primary metric (§1, §8) — §1 says "alfred'i alfred yapan şey kişiliğidir", but no SM checks the personality. SM-C3 says "Kişilik, kısalık ve doğrulukla ölçülür", yet nothing measures brevity or accuracy. *Fix:* Promote a check to an SM. For example: "Addendum'daki diyalog senaryolarında yanıtlar FR-1 sınırlarına uyar ve FR-2'ye göre hipotez/sonuç ayrımı yapar."
- **[low]** SM-3 is vague (§8) — "her gün gerçekten kullanılır" has no threshold. *Fix:* For example, "İşlem kaydında 14 gün boyunca her gün en az 1 Görev."

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
Most FRs carry testable bullets. FR-9 lists the exact contents of a failure message. FR-12's fail-closed and `sudo` rules are crisp. FR-15 spells out what does not count as confirmation. FR-18 gives numeric thresholds (80% / 100% / 15 Tur). The gaps sit where they hurt most: the safety classifier, the timing of the demo, and an internal contradiction in the personality acceptance references.

### Findings
- **[high]** FR-12 does not say how compound shell commands are classified (§4.5 FR-12, table) — The shell Tool accepts arbitrary command strings. The table classifies single commands (`cat` = 0, `rm` = 2). But there is no rule for pipes, `&&`/`;` chains, `$(...)` substitution, or redirection (`cat x > config.yml` is an overwrite hidden behind a Kademe 0 verb). This is the core promise of the product ("geri alınamayan her şey, güvenlik kelimesi olmadan yapılmaz"), and as written an engineer cannot test it. *Fix:* Add bullets to FR-12: "Bileşik komutlar en yüksek Kademe'li parçasıyla sınıflandırılır; `>`/`>>` yönlendirmesi dosya yazma sayılır; ayrıştırılamayan komut Kademe 2'dir."
- **[high]** FR-1's own acceptance references break FR-1 (§4.1 FR-1 vs addendum "Örnek diyaloglar") — FR-1 says voice replies are "en fazla 3 cümle" and that "Addendum'daki örnek diyaloglar kabul testlerinde referans olarak kullanılır." But the canonical Teşhis reply has 6 sentences, the Onay reply has 5, and both failure examples have 5–6. A test author cannot satisfy both. *Fix:* Do one of two things. Either mark the addendum examples as CLI/detail-mode references, or trim them to 3 sentences. Or loosen FR-1, for example "teşhis ve onay yanıtlarında en fazla 5 cümle."
- **[high]** The voice demo has no latency budget (§4.2 FR-3, SM-1, UJ-1) — UJ-1 packs 6 exchanges, including a multi-Tur diagnosis and a fix-and-verify, into "60 saniyelik demo", and SM-1 requires it "tek seferde ve kurgusuz". FR-3 has no bound on push-to-talk-release-to-first-audio, and nothing bounds diagnosis duration. Whether SM-1 is achievable depends on the STT/GPU answer (§9 Q3). *Fix:* Add a bound to FR-3, for example "basit isteklerde tuş bırakıldıktan sonra ilk sesli yanıt ≤ 3 sn". Or relax SM-1 to "UJ-1 kesintisiz çalışır; süre hedefi ~60–90 sn."
- **[medium]** UJ-1 step 4 probably hits fail-closed (§2.3 UJ-1, §4.5 table) — Fixing a port mapping usually means recreating the container (`docker compose up -d`, `docker rm` + `run`). That is in no table row, so under FR-12 it is "bilinmeyen her komut" and becomes Kademe 2. The demo would then ask for a safety word at step 4, which UJ-1 does not show. *Fix:* Add compose recreate/up to Kademe 1 explicitly, or show the safety word at step 4.
- **[medium]** "Birebir" matching of the safety word is undefined for voice input (§4.5 FR-15) — STT output varies in case, punctuation and diacritics ("Yeşil." / "yesil"). *Fix:* Define normalization, for example "büyük/küçük harf ve noktalama yok sayılır; Türkçe karakterler korunur". Also say how the word is generated (for example "Türkçe ortak kelime listesinden, STT'nin güvenilir tanıdığı kelimeler").
- **[medium]** "Somut bir risk" in FR-14 is model judgment with no criterion (§4.5 FR-14) — The only example is "Aktif bağlantılar var". As written it cannot be tested or reproduced. *Fix:* List the concrete risk checks the deterministic code performs for Kademe 1 Eylem'ler, for example active connections before a restart, or uncommitted changes before a commit. Leave anything else optional.
- **[medium]** FR-16's "anında" has no bound and no rule for child processes (§4.5 FR-16) — §9 Q2 already flags the mechanism. But the requirement itself should state the outcome. *Fix:* "Kısayoldan sonra ≤ 1 sn içinde: devam eden alt süreçler sonlandırılır, Claude çağrısı iptal edilir, durdurma işlem kaydına yazılır, alfred 'Durdum, efendim' der."
- **[medium]** Failure of the LLM or the network is not covered (§4.3 FR-9) — FR-9 handles Tool errors. There is no behavior for a Claude API outage, a timeout or a rate limit mid-Görev, even though the PRD depends entirely on that API. *Fix:* Add a bullet to FR-9: "Claude API'ye ulaşılamazsa alfred Görev'i durdurur, yarım kalan Eylem'leri raporlar, yeniden denemez."
- **[low]** "En popüler video" is ambiguous (§4.4 FR-11) — Most views overall? Top search result? *Fix:* Pin it to "arama sonucundaki ilk müzik videosu" or similar, consistent with whatever §9 Q4 decides.

## Scope honesty — strong
Omissions are explicit. FR-3 has its own "Kapsam dışı" line with a cross-reference. §7.2 stages deferred work by version. §2.2 names who the product is not for. §5 states the accepted privacy risk outright. The open-items density is low (6 Open Questions, 0 `[ASSUMPTION]`, 0 `[NOTE FOR PM]`) and appropriate for a hobby PRD. §0 states that all assumptions were confirmed and turned into requirements, which is a legitimate choice.

### Findings
- **[medium]** Masking policy appears contradictory (§5 vs FR-19) — §5 says "Yasak klasör ve maskeleme yoktur" (file contents go to Claude unmasked). FR-19 says "Gizli bilgiler … kayda girmeden önce maskelenir." Both are probably intended (mask the local log, don't mask the API payload), but a reader sees a contradiction. The addendum also says "Ajanın okuyabildiği alanda gizli bilgi bulunmasın", yet alfred's own API key lives on that machine. *Fix:* Narrow §5 to "Claude'a gönderilen içerikte maskeleme yoktur". Add a non-goal or constraint covering where alfred's API key lives, for example "alfred kendi API anahtarını okuyabileceği dizinlerde tutmaz."
- **[low]** `git push` at Kademe 1 sits against the "geri alınamaz" rule (§4.5 table) — A normal push publishes to a remote and cannot really be undone (on a public repo it cannot be unseen). That is defensible for a solo developer, but it is an unstated judgment call. *Fix:* Add half a sentence of rationale ("normal push, geçmişi bozmadığı için Kademe 1").

## Downstream usability — adequate
This PRD feeds `bmad-architecture` and `bmad-create-epics-and-stories` (§0), so this dimension matters. Strengths: a real glossary (§3) whose capitalized terms (Görev, Tur, Eylem, Kademe) are used consistently in most places. FR/UJ/SM IDs are contiguous and unique. Each feature section says which UJ it realizes. Every UJ has a named protagonist (Emre). The cross-references (`addendum.md`, `research-digest.md`, the brief path) resolve.

### Findings
- **[medium]** "Konuşma" collides with its everyday meaning of speech (§3 vs FR-3, §7.1 M3) — The glossary defines Konuşma as a conversation session. FR-3 uses "Konuşma metne çevrilir" to mean speech, and M3 says "Türkçe konuşma girişi". Story extraction may tie session-memory rules (FR-5) to the STT pipeline. *Fix:* Use "ses" or "söylenen" for speech in FR-3 and M3 ("Söylenen metne çevrilir"). Or rename the glossary term to "Oturum".
- **[low]** Feature sections for cost and the log lack complete UJ/SM traceability (§4.6, §8 SM-1) — §4.6 cites no UJ, although UJ-3 reports spend. SM-1 lists FR-3, 6, 10, 11 and 15, but UJ-1 also exercises FR-1, FR-7 and FR-8. *Fix:* Add "UJ-3'ü destekler" to §4.6 and FR-7/FR-8 to SM-1.

## Shape fit — strong
The PRD has the right shape for a hobby/solo, chain-top product with real UX (voice and personality). It has three short UJs with a named protagonist, and the 60-second demo journey is load-bearing because it doubles as SM-1. It uses a capability-spec style for the FRs, avoids enterprise NFR sections, and keeps depth (dialogues, research, cost tactics) in the addendum. Its length sits at the top of the 2–4 page target, and that is justified by the safety model. It is neither over- nor under-formalized.
